What the AI referral workbook actually says

Every formula in the workbook was recomputed from the Sessions rows and compared against its cached result. The arithmetic is clean. Three of the numbers it presents as headlines are not, because they divide two populations that don’t cover the same days — or the same AI assistants.

How this was checked. Every SUMIFS, SUBTOTAL, AVERAGEIFS, MINIFS/MAXIFS, share and CTR cell was rebuilt independently from the 404 Sessions rows and compared to the value Excel had cached. 104 page rows, 121 day rows, 18 week rows, 4 referrer rows: zero disagreements. Date parts, ISO weeks, weekday and weekend flags, source-coverage flags and the de-duplication flag in Sessions!J all reproduce exactly. No error cells; the six post-2007 functions carry their _xlfn. prefixes correctly, and the totals use SUBTOTAL so the CTR really does reflow when you filter. The problems below are about what the numbers mean, not whether they add up.

Findings

Six things to fix before any of this goes in a deck

OverstatedWeekly!K19

The last week’s CTR is roughly double the real figure

Week 37 reports 16.0% — 36 sessions ÷ 225 citations. But 19 of those 36 sessions fall on 9–11 September, three days where Bing supplied no citations at all. The numerator covers five days; the denominator covers two. On the two days both sources cover, the week is 7.6%. The same bug runs the other way in Week 20, where 14 May contributes 123 citations and no sessions: 0.77% reported against 1.47% coverage-matched. The README tells the reader to “filter to both before comparing the two series” — the Weekly tab never does.

Restrict Weekly!E:F to Daily!Q = "both", or drop the two partial weeks from any trend chart.

OverstatedREADME!B8, Pages!K106

The headline 4.81% carries the same mismatch

443 sessions run 15 May – 11 Sep; 9,202 page-level citations run 14 May – 8 Sep. Restricted to the 117 days both sources cover, it is 424 ÷ 10,342 = 4.10%. Choosing the daily citation total instead (README!B10) gives 4.23%, and dropping the 30 July spike gives 4.49% — so the “overall CTR” moves between 4.10% and 4.81% purely on which denominator and window you pick. That spread is wider than most of the week-to-week movement the workbook is being used to show.

Wrong premiseREADME!B9, Pages!L106

The “Copilot-only, apples-to-apples” CTR is not apples-to-apples

It divides 3 Copilot sessions by all 9,202 Bing citations — and those citations come from Copilot plus Bing AI summaries plus Microsoft’s partner integrations. The numerator is one surface, the denominator is three, so 0.03% understates the true Copilot rate by an unknown multiple. With n = 3 sessions across 2 pages it carries no signal either way. The README presents this as the trustworthy version of the headline; it is the least trustworthy number in the workbook.

Either split Bing’s citation export by surface, or delete the row. Don’t caveat it — a caveated 0.03% still gets quoted.

Read with carePages!K2:K105

Page-level CTR is meaningless below about 100 citations

Of the 59 pages with both citations and landings, 7 exceed 50% and two exceed 100%: “Is my business name my IP?” reads 233% (42 sessions, 18 citations) and “Respond to an opposition” reads 200% (4 sessions, 2 citations). A ratio above 100% is impossible for a real click-through rate and is the clearest available proof that the two populations don’t nest: ChatGPT sends the traffic, Bing counts the citations. The column is fine as a relative signal on high-volume pages and noise everywhere else.

UnderstatedREADME!B30, B24

Two README caveats are milder than the data

The 30 July spike is 609 citations — 7.4× the median day of 82.5, not the “roughly six times” the README states. Excluding it lifts all-period CTR from 4.23% to 4.49%, and July from 2.34% to 2.94%. Separately, the README says “roughly a third of ChatGPT landings would not have matched” without URL normalisation; in fact 281 of 363 ChatGPT rows (77%) have a raw landing URL that differs from its canonical form, and 259 of those resolve to a page that is in the Bing cited set. Normalisation is doing far more work than the note admits — which makes it the single assumption most worth auditing.

MislabelledWeekly!L20, Sessions grain

Two labels don’t describe what the cell computes

Weekly!L20 is headed “CTR (mean of daily CTRs)” but computes the mean of 18 weekly means, weighting a 4-day week like a 7-day one: 5.02% against a true daily mean of 4.99%. Small here, but it is an average of averages wearing another name. And the README describes Sessions as page-by-day-by-referrer grain — 29 key combinations appear twice, separated only by raw_landing_url. Totals are unaffected because SUMIFS sums every matching row, but any join that assumes one row per key will double-count. Pages!R106 is a third case: it sums per-page day counts to 368, which double-counts days — the site saw sessions on 98 distinct dates.

Checks outREADME!B29, B33

The caveats the README gets exactly right

The two Bing exports really do disagree: 10,465 by day against 9,202 by page — 1,263 citations, 12.07% of the daily total, which matches the README’s “about 12 percent” precisely. The truncated-link claim is exact too: two ChatGPT sessions (30 July and 1 September) landed on …create-ip- with the -0 suffix cut off, and served “Page not found”; three more raw URLs are cut mid-?utm parameter. The page counts (88 cited, 75 landed), all four referrer totals, the distinct-page counts and the source-coverage flags all reproduce exactly.
